## What it is

Upstream is a public-record map of the people, companies, labs, agencies, think tanks and investors shaping artificial intelligence, and of how they connect: who leads what, who funds whom, who supplies whom, who regulates whom, who used to work where. Each connection carries a source. Each stated position is the speaker's own dated public words, scored on a defined question, with the link.

## The six questions

Every person on the map who has said something on the record is scored on one or more of these, from -2 to +2, always with a dated quote and a source. Gray means "no sourced position found", never "neutral": a position is never inferred from an employer.

1. **Open weights.** Should the most capable AI models be released openly?
2. **Regulation.** Should governments set binding rules for frontier AI, including safety rules and export controls?
3. **Cyber access.** Should AI with strong hacking ability go only to vetted defenders?
4. **Pace.** Does moving fast matter more than slowing down for safety?
5. **Training data.** Should AI training on published work require permission or payment?
6. **Military.** Should AI be used in weapons and warfighting?

## What you can do in the map

- **Network, globe and stack views.** See the whole web flat, on a globe by country, or as layers from compute up to policy.
- **Colour by question.** Recolour the entire map by how everyone stands on one of the six questions; the split view lines people up from one end of the spectrum to the other.
- **Filter by connection type.** Money, former jobs, schools, lawsuits, contracts, board seats, family.
- **Timeline.** 123 dated events, replayable, from the early labs to the 2026 policy fights.
- **Money.** Funding, lobbying and political-money charts, each figure linked to its filing or report.
- **27 guided tours.** From "Start here" to "Follow the capital", "The Pentagon and the labs", "Lobbying and the PACs", "The revolving door" and "Beyond America".
- **Profiles.** Any entry opens a sheet with its role, its sourced facts, its statements with dates and links, and the other editions it appears in.

## How it was checked

Research ran in five rounds, each one checked by someone who had not built it. Three researchers gathered the first round of statements (security, compute and policy). A separate fact-checker then checked every first-round statement against its source: 71 held up as written, 11 were corrected (wrong dates, scores that read too strongly, and open-letter text credited as a signer's own words), and 7 were removed as off-question or unconfirmable. A third round added capital, defense, society and world layers, and every new statement from the second and third rounds went through a second independent check. A fourth round added schools, public voices, science and health, the builders and actors outside the US, plus about 650 new connections; two more independent fact-checkers checked all 46 new statements, 140 connections (every investment, grant, lawsuit, contract, board seat and former job, plus a sample of the rest), 36 roles, 40 profile facts and 14 surprising recent claims. A fifth round added funding, lobbying and political money plus a dated timeline of 123 events, with three more independent checks: every new money link and bill position (127 held up, 11 corrected), every timeline event (89 held up, 26 corrected, 8 confirmed missing events added), and a role audit of all 120 earlier people (8 roles and 11 facts updated).

The full method, the counts and the caveats are in the map's own **About this map** panel, behind the "How it was checked" link on the entry screen.

## What it leaves out

Only public figures acting in their public roles. No personal details beyond the professional record. Coverage is weighted toward the US and UK. Third-party summaries are never used as the source for a quote. Gray is absence of evidence, not neutrality.
