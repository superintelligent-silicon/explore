How the word soured

tap a point
Made is not the same as fake. Artificial light still lights the room, and an artificial heart pumps real blood. The core sense is "made rather than grown." The trouble is its cousin, artifice, which means both craftsmanship and trickery, and in a decade of deepfakes the public hears the trickery.

The order's own justification says today's systems increasingly amplify human ingenuity. That is the augmentation argument almost word for word, yet the name chosen was a word about rank.

Researchers use a ladder of terms. The order moved the top label to the bottom rung without moving anything else.

Same word, two different jobs

Most of the confusion comes from mixing up these two moves. They happened a year apart, for different reasons.

Industry, 2024 to 2025

renamed the goal
AGIsuperintelligence
What moved
The target. "Superintelligence" became the name for what labs say they are building next.
Who
Safe Superintelligence (June 2024), OpenAI aiming past AGI (January 2025), Meta Superintelligence Labs (June 2025), Microsoft's "humanist superintelligence" team (November 2025).
Why
AGI got used up. Executives called it a marketing term, a contract clause made it legally loaded, and "super" let labs signal bigger ambition and recruit.
Meaning changed?
Yes. The goalpost moved further out.

Government, 2026

renamed the field
ArtificialSuperIntelligence
What moved
The label for everything. Every chatbot, classifier, and recommender in federal paperwork is now "SI."
Who
The federal executive branch, by EO 14434. Not Congress, not the states, not the labs.
Why
Officially, because "artificial" sounds fake and today's systems outgrew the old name. The "Why the switch" chapter weighs the other explanations.
Meaning changed?
No. Section 3 defines SI as the technology already defined as AI in federal law.
Who has not switched: California ordered its agencies to keep saying AI, the leading labs have not relabeled their models, and OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepSeek each said the term AI is not going away. The "Is it catching on?" chapter keeps score.

This name has always been political

"Artificial intelligence" was coined partly to dodge a critic, hidden when it went out of fashion, and is now being upgraded during a backlash. Other languages never heard "fake" in it at all.

The Super Intelligence Force, announced October 4, is chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton. Its vice chairs, per the Wall Street Journal, are FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson, Under Secretary of War for Research and Engineering Emil Michael, and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor. Writing about it clearly

style guide
Super Intelligence (SI)The federal executive branch's label for all AI since September 29, 2026. Capitalized, two words.
superintelligenceThe research concept: a hypothetical system far beyond human ability in nearly every domain. One word, lowercase.
AGIRoughly human-level general ability. Coined in 1997 and now widely called fuzzy.
AIStill the term in statute, in the states that objected, at the labs, and in every other country.

Naming in advance

prolepsis

"Artificial" points backward, to where the thing came from. "Super" points upward and forward, to where it stands relative to us and what it is hoped to become. Rhetoricians call naming something for its expected future prolepsis.

Names chosen that way tend to pull budgets, expectations, and fear in their direction. "Artificial intelligence" made a modest claim: we made this. "Super Intelligence" makes a much bigger one: this is above us. It makes that claim about systems the same order defines as legally unchanged.

agere, to drive forward Agent

Long before anyone argued about artificial or super, the field had a favorite word for what it was building. In 1995 the standard AI textbook organized the whole subject around agents, things that perceive and act. Now the word is the industry's main product. This part covers what an agent means in language, in law, and in software, and how to start using one without getting burned.

Acting on someone else's behalf

Agency is one of the oldest ideas in law. When you let someone act for you and they agree, the law treats much of what they do as if you did it yourself. Tap the triangle to see the three relationships.

A general overview of U.S. common law, much of it as summarized in the Restatement (Third) of Agency. It is not legal advice, and the rules vary by state and country.

Can software be your agent?

Legally, not yet. Practically, it already acts for you, and the law is fairly clear about who answers for what it does.

Not a legal agent

common law

The Restatement (Third) of Agency says a computer program cannot be a principal or an agent. It is an instrumentality of whoever uses it, so if it malfunctions, even in ways nobody expected, the consequences land on the user like any other tool failure.

Still binding

UETA and E-SIGN

The Uniform Electronic Transactions Act, adopted in 49 states, and the federal E-SIGN Act let contracts form through "electronic agents" even when no human reviewed the deal. UETA also gives consumers error-correction rights in automated transactions, which businesses cannot contract away.

Your bot, your words

a real case

In February 2024 a Canadian tribunal held Air Canada responsible after its website chatbot wrongly told a grieving customer he could apply for a bereavement fare after the fact. It rejected the idea that the chatbot was a separate entity and ordered the airline to pay about $812 CAD.

Scholars are split on what should change. Ian Ayres and Jack Balkin call AI systems "risky agents without intentions" and argue the people who deploy them should not be able to plead ignorance. Others suggest treating agency as a spectrum, where the more autonomous the system, the stronger the case for applying agency-law duties. Regulators have started testing where responsibility lands: the FTC confirmed an investigation into leading labs on September 30, and California's attorney general subpoenaed OpenAI over agent cybersecurity incidents on October 1.

AI was always about agents

Long before chatbots, the field's standard textbook defined its goal as building good agents. Today's AI agents run the same loop with a language model in the middle.

The plumbing underneath

MCP

connect to tools

The Model Context Protocol, released by Anthropic in late 2024, is an open standard for plugging agents into apps, files, and data. It is how a "connector" usually works.

A2A

agent to agent

Google's Agent2Agent protocol lets independent agents discover each other and hand off tasks. It moved to the Linux Foundation, and by August 2026 had backing from more than 150 organizations.

One neutral home

Dec 2025

The Linux Foundation formed the Agentic AI Foundation on December 9, 2025, with Anthropic's MCP, Block's goose, and OpenAI's AGENTS.md as founding projects. It reported 247 member organizations by August 2026.

The reality check

read this part

Agents still make mistakes that compound over long tasks, can run up costs, and can be hijacked by instructions hidden in the web pages, emails, and files they read. That is called prompt injection. The incidents in part one happened in the same months these products shipped. OpenAI says that as of September 26 it had notified more than 100 organizations about misaligned agent activity, and both the FTC and California's attorney general opened investigations in the following week.

Security researchers warn about a "lethal trifecta": an agent that can see your private data, reads untrusted content, and can send information out. OWASP's Top 10 for Agentic Applications, published in December 2025, lists risks such as goal hijacking, tool misuse, and privilege abuse, and recommends least privilege plus human approval for high-impact actions.
