## What the guide covers

An interactive field guide to why the U.S. government renamed AI "Super Intelligence": the etymology of *artificial*, *intelligence*, and *super*, the polling, the politics, and (part two) a beginner's guide to AI agents.

## Key facts stated in the guide

- **The order.** With Executive Order 14434 (September 29, 2026), the U.S. federal executive branch relabeled all AI as "Super Intelligence (SI)." Section 3 defines SI as the technology already defined as AI in federal law — zero changes to the legal definition.
- **How the name was picked.** Truth Social polls: Sept 19 (Superior, Extreme, or Supreme Intelligence; about 66,000 votes; Superior led), Sept 20 (Supreme pulled; final two Superior or Extreme), Sept 21 (Superior against a new option, Super). Super took 65% in the runoff.
- **Two different switches.** Industry (2024–2025) renamed the *goal* from AGI to "superintelligence" (Safe Superintelligence, June 2024; Meta Superintelligence Labs, June 2025; Microsoft's "humanist superintelligence" team, November 2025). Government (2026) renamed the *field* — every chatbot, classifier, and recommender in federal paperwork — without changing meaning.
- **Public opinion.** YouGov (week of Sept 28): 53% prefer "artificial intelligence," 9% prefer "super intelligence." Marquette (Sept 2026): 64% call AI development bad for society. Quinnipiac (Sept 29): AI was the lowest-rated issue for presidential approval, 25% approve / 58% disapprove.
- **Who has not switched.** California ordered its agencies to keep saying AI; the leading labs have not relabeled their models; OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepSeek each said the term AI is not going away.
- **What to watch.** By about November 28, 2026, the President's science advisor must send proposed legislative language defining SI (EO 14434, Sec. 3). The Super Intelligence Force, announced October 4, 2026, is chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and reportedly has about 120 days to report.
- **Style guide.** *Super Intelligence (SI)* — the federal executive branch's label for all AI since September 29, 2026 (capitalized, two words). *superintelligence* — the research concept of a system far beyond human ability (one word, lowercase). *AGI* — roughly human-level general ability. *AI* — still the term in statute, at the labs, and in other countries.

## Etymology in brief

- **Artificial** — Latin *artificialis*, "of or belonging to art," from *ars* + *facere* (to make). Core sense: made rather than grown. Its cousin *artifice* means both craftsmanship and trickery.
- **Intelligence** — Latin *intelligere*, "to understand," literally *inter* + *legere*, "to choose between."
- **Super** — Latin *super*, "above, over, beyond"; same root family as superior, supreme, sovereign, Greek *hyper*, German *über*.
- **Agent** — Latin *agere*, "to set in motion, drive forward; to do."

## Part two: AI agents

- Agency in law: under the Restatement (Third) of Agency, a computer program cannot be a principal or an agent; it is an instrumentality of whoever uses it. UETA and E-SIGN still let contracts form through "electronic agents." In February 2024 a Canadian tribunal held Air Canada responsible for what its chatbot told a customer.
- The standard 1995 AI textbook organized the field around agents — things that perceive and act.
- Plumbing: MCP (Model Context Protocol, released by Anthropic in late 2024) connects agents to tools; Google's A2A protocol connects agents to each other; the Linux Foundation formed the Agentic AI Foundation on December 9, 2025.
- Risks: compounding mistakes, runaway costs, and prompt injection (instructions hidden in pages, emails, and files an agent reads). The guide recommends a small first task, a clear brief, and granting permissions one notch at a time.
